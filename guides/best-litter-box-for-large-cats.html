What matters for a big cat

01

Open top or enclosed

An open basin has no ceiling, so cats that feel boxed in may take to it more easily. Enclosed boxes have a fixed doorway.

02

The size of the opening

The three here range from 10.51 x 10.74 in to 15.75 x 15.75 in. Measure your cat's shoulders and chest and compare. The Neakasa page lists only the entry height (13.86 in), not the width or depth.

03

The weight limit

Limits here are 22, 30 and 33 lbs. Pick a box whose limit sits comfortably above your cat's adult weight.

04

Room inside

A big cat needs space to turn, dig and squat without bumping the sifting parts. The makers don't publish interior measurements, so if you can, check a unit in person or read the manual before you buy.

Which one for which cat

A cat near 30 lbs or more, or one that hates enclosed spaces: the Neakasa M1 Plus. It has the highest weight limit and no roof. It's also the cheapest and the loudest, at 50 dB.

A large cat that's fine with an enclosed box: the Litter-Robot 5. The biggest doorway of the enclosed models and a 30 lb limit, at the highest price.

A sturdy cat under about 20 lbs: the PETKIT Max Pro 2 can work, and it's the quietest at 35 dB. Check the 10.51 x 10.74 in opening against your cat's shoulders first.

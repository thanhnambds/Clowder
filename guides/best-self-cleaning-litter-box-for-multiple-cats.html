Why more cats change the math

01

The bin fills sooner

Every "days between emptying" figure below is for one cat. With three cats, expect to empty it far more often.

02

Cats overlap

One cat finishes, the cycle starts, and another walks up. A box needs sensors that detect a cat and stop the cycle. All the makers describe sensors built for this, but those are their claims, not test results.

03

Health clues get harder to spot

With several cats sharing a box, it's tough to tell who changed their habits. Some models log visits and weights in an app. That helps, but it doesn't replace watching your cats.

04

Odor builds faster

More use means more strain on filters and bin seals. Plan on replacing liners and filters more often.

One more rule of thumb: Cats.com recommends one litter box per cat plus one extra. An automatic box can take the place of one of those boxes, not all of them. (source)

Questions

Can several cats share one automatic litter box?

Only Whisker states a cat count: up to five for the Litter-Robot 5. The Neakasa and PETKIT pages we read don't give one. Whatever the box, a second or third cat shortens the time between bin changes.

What sensors do these boxes use?

Lasers plus a weight sensor on the Litter-Robot 5. Six sets of infrared sensors plus four weight sensors on the Neakasa M1 Plus. Twelve sensors on the PETKIT Max Pro 2 and Max 3, and twenty on the Ultra. The makers say these detect a cat and stop the cycle.

Three ways these boxes work

Most motorized litter boxes sift waste in one of three ways.

Rotating drum or globe. The whole chamber turns. Litter falls through a screen, waste drops into a drawer below, and the drum levels the bed. The Litter-Robot 5 and the PETKIT work this way.

Open-top basin. A curved sifter sweeps through an open tray and pushes waste into a bin. Your cat can see out the whole time. The Neakasa M1 Plus works this way.

Rake. A comb drags waste across a flat bed into a covered trap. These usually pair with crystal litter. We don't compare any here.

What to check before you buy

01

The opening and the weight limit

Measure your cat's shoulders and compare them with the opening. A cat near the top of a model's weight range may feel cramped in a smaller doorway. Also check the minimum weight if you have a small cat or a kitten.

02

The sensors

Look for more than one way of detecting a cat, such as weight plus lasers or infrared. All three makers say their sensors are built to stop a cycle when a cat is detected. Those are the makers' claims, not test results.

03

The bin

The "days between emptying" figures are for one cat. Add a second and expect to empty it sooner.

04

The litter

Most rotating boxes are made for clumping clay litter. Crystal and pellet litters may not sift well. Check the maker's litter and liner guidance.

WHISKER • ROTATING GLOBE

Litter-Robot 5: the big name, with the big price

Reference Model

Whisker's Litter-Robot 5 is the box other brands get compared with. It is an enclosed globe with the largest opening of the three, and Whisker rates it for cats from 3 to 30 lbs and up to five cats. It uses multiple lasers plus a weight sensor to detect a cat. Whisker doesn't list a noise level.

It is also the most expensive, from $799, and the biggest, at 22 x 27 in with a height of 29.9 in.

A good fit if: you have a large cat or several cats, or you want the roomiest opening.

Think twice if: the budget is tight, floor space is limited, or you want a published noise figure.

NEAKASA • OPEN-TOP BASIN

Neakasa M1 Plus: the budget pick for big cats

Partner Link

Neakasa's M1 Plus skips the closed drum. It is an open basin, so there is no door to squeeze through. It lists a 13.86 in entry height but not the width or depth. Cats from 2.2 to 33 lbs are covered, the widest range of the three. The 11.23 L bin lasts about 14 days for one cat, according to Neakasa.

It was $379.99 when we checked (regular $549.99), and $399.99 on Amazon. It's also the loudest of the three, at 50 dB. Neakasa lists 6 sets of infrared sensors and 4 weight sensors, and a 1-year US/Canada warranty.

A good fit if: you have a big cat that dislikes enclosed boxes, or you want the lowest price.

Think twice if: noise matters in your home.

Questions

Are automatic litter boxes safe for kittens?

Not for very young ones. These three list minimum weights of 2.2, 3 and 3.3 lbs. A kitten below that is outside what the sensors were designed for. Check your model's number first.

What happens if my cat walks in during a cycle?

The makers describe sensors that detect a cat and stop the cycle. The types differ: lasers plus weight on the Litter-Robot 5, infrared plus weight on the Neakasa, and 12 integrated sensors on the PETKIT. Keep sensors clean, as the manuals suggest.

What litter do I need?

Most rotating models are built for clumping clay litter. Crystal and pellet litters may not sift properly. Check the maker's guidance.
